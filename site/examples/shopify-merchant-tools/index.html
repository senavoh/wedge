Verdict

PIVOT

The pain is real: small POS retailers lost Stocky on Aug 31, 2026, and they say Shopify's native POs take triple the time. But don't build another Stocky replacement app. A free, 4.9-rated one (Stockroom) and more than ten cheap apps already cover it, and these shops resist new monthly fees. The pivot is to change the channel and price: sell a one-time $199 setup service on top of the free tools, and run the 7-day test before building anything. Read-only Stocky access ends Nov 29, so test now.

Stocky replacement app for small POS retailers

Strongest pain in the scout: Stocky is rated 3.0 with 28% one-star reviews and was shut down, merchants call native POs brutally inadequate, and some are leaving Shopify POS. But Stockroom is free and unlimited, rated 4.9, and more than ten other apps pitch in the same threads at $0 to $20. Shops cap spend at $100/mo and resent paying for what was free.

  • “Stocky has the potential to be a fantastic app, but it’s been completely abandoned.”
    shopify-app-store · 2025-10-30 verified
  • “Shopify has confirmed that Stocky will no longer be available after August 31, 2026 , and that it was delisted from the Shopify App Store on February 2, 2026”
    shopify-community · 2026-02-20 verified

Wholesale ordering for small makers

Merchants lose wholesale orders when pricing isn't applied or net terms fail, and they pay $35 to $149/mo for apps. But Shopify B2B now runs on Basic and up, and pricing logic touches checkout and themes. Not deep-dived; needs its own run.

  • “Leaving groups of my customer base unable to order due to special pricing not being applied.”
    shopify-app-store · 2026-06-01 verified
  • “We have lost so, so much on using this app - both on the app cost itself but also lost orders for customers being annoyed of the constant problems.”
    shopify-app-store · 2024-10-30 verified

Subscriptions for small merchants

Recharge users complain about no small tier, fees above 10% of recurring revenue, and lock-in. But Seal is free up to 50 subscriptions with 0% fees, Shopify Subscriptions is free, and recurring billing is high-stakes for a 10-hour-a-week builder.

  • “there is no tier for small businesses”
    shopify-app-store · 2025-12-09 verified

The case against

A separate critic pass argued against this. Here is what it found.

Risk: A free incumbent (Stockroom) and ten-plus cheap apps already own the app idea.

Answer: Unresolved.

Risk: The window is closing: Stocky's API stopped Aug 31, and read-only access ends Nov 29, 2026.

Answer: Unresolved.

Risk: The pain is real but these shops resist paying for what used to be free.

Answer: Unresolved.

Risk: Shopify killed Stocky itself and can change native POs at any time.

Answer: Native POs are still called brutally inadequate (e7, e8), and a setup service works on whichever tool Shopify pushes.

Risk: Free migration tools and vendor walkthroughs already exist.

Answer: They move files; merchants are still asking months later how to map vendors, print labels and train staff (e15, e16, e34).

Risk: Copyable in a week by any vendor or freelancer.

Answer: True; this is a side-income service, not a moat. The edge is being a real merchant who answers in the threads.

Risk: The community threads are full of vendor pitches, so another pitch reads as spam.

Answer: Merchants there ask direct questions (e16). Answer first, disclose last.

Risk: Touching a shop's inventory data can wreck its counts.

Answer: Work from exports, test on one location, no bulk writes without sign-off.

10

7-day test

One experiment, one pass bar. Run it before writing code.

Post one help thread, answer 8 to 10 open questions in the Stocky threads, and message 10 named Stocky reviewers; point all of it at the landing page with a $99 founding offer.

Pass bar (7 days): 3 shops pay (or put down a $50 deposit), or 10 booked calls from different shops

  • Day 1: domain, landing page, Stripe link, booking page
  • Day 2: post the export checklist thread
  • Day 3: answer 4 open thread questions
  • Day 4: message 10 named Stocky reviewers
  • Day 5: answer 4 more threads, follow up on replies
  • Day 6: run calls and ask for the $99
  • Day 7: count payments, calls and objections; decide

Kill it if

  • 0 payments and fewer than 4 booked calls after 7 days
  • More than half of calls end with 'I'll just use Stockroom myself'
  • Moderators remove posts as promotion
  • Inbound questions in the Stocky threads stop after Nov 29
  • The first 3 setups each take more than 8 hours
