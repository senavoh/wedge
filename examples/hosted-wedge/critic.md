- **HIGH: a free, MIT-licensed tool already does this exact source mix.** Feedback Synthesis MCP covers the same three non-Reddit sources and returns evidence-linked clusters for agents. "Synthesize GitHub Issues, Hacker News threads, and App Store reviews into ranked pain clusters with evidence links." (https://claudemarketplaces.com/mcp/sapph1re/feedback-synthesis-mcp); "License MIT About Customer feedback intelligence MCP — synthesize GitHub Issues, HN, and App Store reviews into ranked pain clusters." (https://github.com/sapph1re/feedback-synthesis-mcp). Together with Wedge itself being open source, that means two free agent-native options before the hosted version ships. Rebuttal: the repo shows "Stars 0 stars" (same URL), so nobody uses it yet. It is a substitute, not a proven market. The per-quote re-check is still unique (competitors.md "Thin" row). Partly rebutted.
- **HIGH: GripeFind already owns the "no-Reddit, ex-GummySearch" pitch at the same price.** File ids c1-c3, c13 ($29/mo, "Coming from GummySearch?"). Rebuttal: Unresolved. No source shows GripeFind failing or its users complaining.
- **HIGH: the GummySearch founder is coming back for the same audience.** "Fed, the maker of GummySearch, will be building more market research products in 2026." (https://gummysearch.com/closing-time). GummySearch also links its former users to its own alternatives page: "You can go to our alternatives page to find other tools that might help you." (same URL). The 135k-user list (e4) belongs to him, not to Wedge. Rebuttal: Unresolved. Nothing found showing what he built or whether it avoids Reddit.
- **HIGH: general deep-research chat is already a known free-or-$20 substitute for review mining.** "Review Mining Enter the savior of time-strapped product managers everywhere: ChatGPT's "Deep Research" feature paired with a whole lotta customer reviews." and "I tried it with PODS moving service and got back insights so specific, it was like I'd painstakingly interviewed 50 customers and then meticulously distilled my findings into an actionable guide." (same URL). Rebuttal: Wedge's edge is quotes that are re-checked against the source, and pains.md e21-e28 show users distrust flattering, made-up AI output. No source shows a deep-research user complaining about fake quotes in review mining specifically. Partly rebutted.
- **HIGH (structural, already visible): the anchor channel dies the way GummySearch did, and "no Reddit" also means losing the richest source.** "While GummySearch has been working with Reddit to obtain a commercial license for compliant access to Reddit's Data API, we were not able to reach an agreement that aligns with Reddit's Data API Usage policies." (https://gummysearch.com/final-chapter). Also in files: e12, e14-e20. Rebuttal: Wedge avoids Reddit by design, so this risk lands on competitors. The cost is weaker data for the people who loved GummySearch *because* of Reddit. Unresolved whether buyers accept non-Reddit sources.
- **MEDIUM: Apple scraping terms for App Store reviews.** Apple's guideline: "The clause states: “Apps may use approved Apple RSS feeds such as the iTunes Store RSS feed, but may not scrape any information from Apple sites (e.g." (https://conductatlas.com/platform/apple/apple-app-store-review-guidelines/provision/CA-P-019006/apps-may-not-scrape-apple-site-information/). That rule binds submitted apps, not web services, so it's medium. A hosted service hitting Apple endpoints at volume could still get rate-limited or have the feed removed. A secondhand claim that Apple deprecated the review feed in 2026 could not be confirmed. Rebuttal: Unresolved.
- **LOW: GitHub terms.** API collection is allowed. "Scraping does not refer to the collection of information through our API." The ban is on spam and resale: "You may not use information from the Service (whether scraped, collected through our API, or obtained otherwise) for spamming purposes, including for the purposes of sending unsolicited emails to users or selling personal information, such as to recruiters, headhunters, and job boards." (https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-acceptable-use-policies). Quoting issues for research fits the rules. Rebutted, unless the service stores usernames or emails.

- **LOW-MEDIUM: "tried and died" check came up empty.** Searches for shutdowns of hosted pain-research or idea-validation tools (web plus HN Algolia) found no specific dead tool other than GummySearch, which died of Reddit licensing, not lack of demand. That is weak evidence the category supports paid tools. It is also likely survivorship: zero-comment launches (c13-c15) fade away without postmortems.

Net: nothing here rescues the KILL in gaps.md. The strongest new reasons are a free MIT tool with the identical source mix, deep research as the free/$20 default, and the GummySearch founder relaunching for the same list.
