Verdict

PIVOT

The pain is real and well documented: on WordPress.org, small organizers and their agencies say events plugin updates break ticket sales, paid support goes silent and renewals jump 50%. But the obvious fix, a hosted checkout that looks native on WordPress, already exists (Ticket Spot, with only 40+ installs; Ticket Tailor's embed). Nonprofits get ticketing free from Zeffy, and the WordPress.org forums forbid promotion, so organizer-direct scores 55. Pivot to WordPress agencies managing many small-org sites (57 on paper, untested) and run the 7-day agency test before building anything.

The gap

The opening competitors leave, backed by what their customers say.

Small organizers who sell tickets from their own WordPress site can't get a checkout that keeps working through updates and reliably delivers tickets at a small, predictable price, because every plugin runs checkout inside their WordPress (where updates break it) and every hosted option charges per ticket or drops an off-brand embed on their page. Critic note: Ticket Spot already targets this exact gap (k1), so the remaining opening is the agency buyer.

The case against

A separate critic pass argued against this. Here is what it found.

Risk: The exact product already exists: Ticket Spot sells a hosted, update-proof WordPress checkout to churches and theatres (k1, k4), and Ticket Tailor claims an embedded WordPress checkout (k5).

Answer: Unresolved.

Risk: A free incumbent covers big segments: Zeffy is free for nonprofits (k7), and Ticket Tailor lets organizers pass fees to buyers (k6).

Answer: Unresolved.

Risk: The pain may not convert to payment: the exact fix has only 40+ WordPress.org installs (k2), and ticket-delivery failures are rare in the corpus.

Answer: Unresolved.

Risk: The channel is closed: WordPress.org forums, where buyers complain, forbid promotion (k8).

Answer: Unresolved.

Risk: Ticketing platforms that held funds failed and left organizers unpaid (k9, k10, k12), so buyers distrust new vendors.

Answer: Direct charges into each client's own Stripe account mean no ticket money ever sits with us (k4 shows the model), and all data is exportable.

Risk: Chargeback and refund liability, plus event-night on-call for a 10-hour-a-week founder.

Answer: Avoid destination charges, where the platform is liable (k11). Use direct charges on clients' own Standard accounts. Event-night on-call is still a real cost to price in.

7-day test

One experiment, one pass bar. Run it before writing code.

Contact 40 WordPress agencies and freelancers who publicly reviewed events plugins (through their own sites), publish two search pages, and run a $50 switch-intent ad test.

Pass bar (7 days): 5 agency calls booked and 2 say they'd pay $29/mo+, or 30 waitlist signups from agency domains

  • Day 1: publish the landing page, vs-page and recovery checklist
  • Day 2: list 40 agencies from public reviews; email 20
  • Day 3: email 20 more; start the $50 ad test
  • Day 4: answer (no links) in agency communities whose rules allow it
  • Days 5 to 6: hold calls
  • Day 7: score against the pass bar

Kill it if

  • Fewer than 3 calls booked from 40 contacts
  • No agency says it would pay a flat agency price
  • Fewer than 10 waitlist signups after 7 days
  • Agencies are happy sending clients to Ticket Tailor or Zeffy
  • Ad CTR under 1% on switch-intent terms
